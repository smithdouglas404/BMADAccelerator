# Ideation techniques the Studio uses

Short cards for the lenses in SKILL.md. Use the question, not the jargon: the person never needs to hear the technique's name unless they ask.

## Analogues (analogous inspiration)
Question: "Who solves a similar problem in a completely different world?"
Look for products that share the shape of the problem (matching two sides, trust between strangers, scheduling scarce people, tracking something until it's done). Name two or three and the one thing to borrow from each. Example: home sharing borrowed listings from classifieds, trust from reviews, and standards from hotels.

## Jobs to be done
Question: "When this comes up today, what do people actually do, and what does it cost them?"
Find the workaround (spreadsheets, texts, phone calls, a person in the middle), its cost (time, money, mistakes, worry), and the moment it hurts most. The product wins by making that moment easy.

## First principles
Question: "What does everyone assume has to be true here, and is it still true?"
List the assumptions the category is built on, then test each: technology, regulation, behaviour or cost may have changed. Ride sharing questioned "only licensed cabs can be hailed"; phones, GPS and ratings changed the answer.

## The value grid (eliminate, reduce, raise, create)
Questions, against today's alternatives:
- Eliminate: what does everyone offer that customers don't value?
- Reduce: what is over-built?
- Raise: what is under-served?
- Create: what has never been offered?
Pick one or two moves per row at most.

## How might we
Turn a problem into an opportunity question: "How might we make a repair request feel finished the moment it's sent?" Write three to five, from narrow to bold, and offer them as numbered prompts.

## Deliberate twists (SCAMPER)
Take the idea and: Substitute a part, Combine with something else, Adapt from elsewhere, Magnify or minify, Put to another use, Eliminate a step, Reverse the order or the roles. Offer the two or three twists that produce something genuinely different.

## The riskiest assumption
Question: "If this one belief is wrong, does the whole idea fall over?"
Name it, say how sure we are, and design the cheapest pilot test (a fake door, a concierge version done by hand, five interviews, a landing page). Put it in the Pilot readiness view as the proof point.

## The press release test (working backwards)
Write the launch headline, one paragraph, and a customer quote as if it shipped. Read it as the customer. If it isn't exciting or isn't clear, keep shaping before building. This is also how the PRFAQ stays honest as the product moves.

## Diverge, then converge
A round produces options (diverge). Then ask which one or two matter most (converge) and go deep. Never leave a round without a choice or a next step.
